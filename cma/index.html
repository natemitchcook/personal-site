What matters most

Three areas worth understanding first

The goal is to learn where mistakes happen and where work gets stuck before choosing software or building anything custom.

01 · Estimating

Catch errors before they become expensive

Where do missed scope, wrong quantities, inconsistent pricing, outdated assumptions, or subcontractor exclusions cause problems? How are estimates reviewed today?

What we'd want to learn: How estimates are prepared, checked, approved, and compared with actual project costs.

02 · Accounting

Reduce manual mistakes and rework

Which parts of invoice handling, coding, reconciliation, project reporting, and approvals create the most corrections or delays?

What we'd want to learn: Where numbers are entered more than once, how discrepancies are caught, and who approves changes.

03 · Outlook

Make email easier to stay on top of

How much time goes into reading, prioritizing, searching, following up, and responding to project email? What messages are most likely to get buried?

What we'd want to learn: How Sam currently organizes Outlook, what requires a response, and what must never be missed.

Across all three

Keep the project story together

Important decisions often live across emails, meetings, spreadsheets, and project files. Understanding those connections could make future work more reliable.

What we'd want to learn: Where the latest facts live, who made decisions, and how people find the reasoning later.

A sensible starting path

Start small and prove what's useful.

STEP 1

See the work as it happens

Walk through a normal day in Outlook, a recent estimate that required corrections, and an accounting task that often creates rework. Understand the current process before changing it.

STEP 2

Choose one contained experiment

Test a low-risk workflow with clear measures of success. Outlook triage and draft preparation may be an approachable first test; estimate checking or accounting exception review could follow once the relevant data is understood.

STEP 3

Measure whether it helps

Compare time spent, errors detected, false alarms, and employee feedback against the current process. Keep financial decisions, payments, and external email sending under human control.

No need to replace existing systems.

The first goal is to understand which tools CMA already uses and whether AI capabilities can work alongside them. There is no assumption that CMA needs a new platform.

For discussion

Thought-starter questions

These are intended to make the first conversation straightforward. Short answers are fine, and a walkthrough of one real example is often more useful than a long explanation.

Estimating: where are the human errors?

What are the most common mistakes? Are they missed scope, incorrect quantities, outdated costs, spreadsheet formulas, or subcontractor bid comparisons? When do you usually discover them? Who reviews an estimate before submission?

Accounting: which mistakes cost the most time or money?

Where do incorrect entries, invoice coding, approvals, reconciliations, or cost reporting go wrong? Are people copying information between systems? How are errors detected and corrected?

Outlook: what would make email feel manageable?

Roughly how many emails arrive each day? What should be prioritized? Which messages require action or follow-up? Would it be useful to summarize threads, prepare draft replies, find decisions, or organize email by project? Is this just for Sam or for a wider team?

Where does information live?

Which software is used for estimating, accounting, and project management? Is the company using Microsoft 365, Outlook, Teams, SharePoint, Excel, shared drives, or other systems? Where are project files and meeting notes kept?

Who would need access?

How many people might benefit initially, and across which roles? Would they need access to different projects, financial information, or email accounts? Who should be allowed to approve actions?

What should we try first?

If one recurring frustration could be reduced over the next month, which would make the biggest difference? How would you know it was working?

A longer-term consideration

Don't lose the reasoning behind decisions.

A project can have hundreds of emails, revisions, estimates, invoices, and conversations. The useful question is not just where the files are, but whether someone can understand what was decided, why it changed, and which information is current.

One project, one understandable history

When a decision affects scope, cost, or schedule, how do people connect it back to the supporting email, document, approval, and project record?

Build on existing habits

How much of that context is already captured in Outlook and the company's current systems? What information is still passed verbally or remembered by individuals?

This is a discovery topic, not a proposed implementation. The immediate priority remains helping people make fewer errors and spend less time on repetitive work.
