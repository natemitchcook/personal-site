How does a project keep its context?

Before discussing AI, software, or automation, I want to understand how CMA's people find information, make decisions, and carry knowledge from estimating through project completion.

Exploratory questions, not a proposal. CMA's internal processes have not been verified. No confidential information should be submitted on this page.

The starting point

Good decisions depend on more than having the right file.

A budget line, a revised drawing, and an email approval might each tell part of the story. What matters is whether someone can understand how they connect: what changed, why, who agreed, which version is current, and what that means for the job today.

INFORMATION

What do we know?

Drawings, estimates, contracts, schedules, invoices, site reports, and correspondence.

DECISIONS

Why did we choose this?

Assumptions, tradeoffs, approvals, exceptions, owners, and the reasoning behind changes.

RELATIONSHIPS

What depends on what?

How a scope change relates to a drawing revision, subcontract, cost code, schedule, and forecast.

CONTINUITY

What happens when people change?

How a new estimator, project manager, or controller reconstructs a project's history.

A useful thought experiment: If a strong project manager joined an active CMA job tomorrow, what would they need to read, who would they need to ask, and how long would it take before they understood the important decisions?
Start with a real example

Follow one project from bid to closeout.

Rather than map the whole company in the abstract, choose a recent project and walk through what happened at each stage. The goal is to understand the real flow of information and responsibility.

1. Before the bid: how did the team build its understanding?

Where did the original scope, drawings, specifications, assumptions, historical costs, subcontractor input, and exclusions come from? Which information was trusted? Who resolved conflicting inputs? What important knowledge was only in someone's head?

2. At handoff: what traveled from estimating to operations?

Which estimate assumptions, risk allowances, exclusions, clarifications, and commitments were formally handed over? What had to be explained verbally? Were there surprises because a decision was understood differently?

3. During execution: how did the project change?

How are RFIs, field conditions, revised drawings, schedule changes, subcontractor conversations, and owner requests connected? When a decision changes scope or cost, how do affected teams learn about it?

4. In accounting: how does the financial picture stay current?

How do budget changes, commitments, invoices, change orders, cost codes, and forecast-to-complete relate? When financial reports differ from a project manager's understanding, how is the discrepancy resolved?

5. At closeout: what knowledge survives?

Are estimating misses, effective decisions, subcontractor performance, change-order lessons, and final actual costs documented in a way the next project team can find and use?

The project knowledge model

Can we reconstruct why a decision was made?

An ontology is simply a shared understanding of the things a business works with and how they relate. We don't need to design one yet. First, we need to learn CMA's own language and relationships.

Project & people

What identifies a project? Which teams, subcontractors, owners, and decision-makers are involved? How do responsibilities change over time?

Scope & documents

How are drawing revisions, specification sections, bid packages, exclusions, and approved changes linked?

Money & commitments

How are estimates, budgets, cost codes, subcontracts, invoices, contingency, and forecast revisions connected?

Decisions & evidence

Who decided what, when, why, using which evidence? Was the decision approved, provisional, superseded, or still unresolved?

How do you record decisions made in meetings or calls?

Are there meeting minutes, action logs, email recaps, or project journals? Which decisions are never written down? Who confirms that a record is accurate?

Can you trace a financial change back to its cause?

For one budget variance, could someone find the relevant drawing, site condition, RFI, subcontractor quote, approval, and accounting entry without asking five people?

How do you know which version of a fact is current?

What happens when a drawing is revised, an estimate assumption changes, or an earlier decision is reversed? Is there a clear source of truth and a history of what changed?

Where does the team rely on institutional memory?

Who knows why particular trade allowances, rates, contract terms, or project approaches were chosen? What knowledge would be lost if that person left?

What should remain isolated to each project?

Which documents and decisions are project-specific, which lessons can be reused company-wide, and who is allowed to see each type of information?

Reference point from prior knowledge-management work: preserve raw sources, an understandable project narrative, a decision register, evidence links, and an explicit record of unknowns or conflicting information. The discovery question is whether CMA already captures these elements, not whether it needs a new platform.
Pre-meeting discovery

Where does everything live today?

This is the one inventory I'd like to understand before the meeting. Product names are useful, but the more important detail is which team uses each system and what information is considered authoritative.

Estimating and preconstruction

Which applications are used for takeoffs, estimates, bid leveling, pricing libraries, and preconstruction approvals? Are estimates kept in a central system, spreadsheets, or both?

Accounting and financial reporting

What ERP or accounting software manages job costing, commitments, AP, WIP, invoicing, and financial reporting? Are there separate project-level trackers?

Project management and field operations

Where do RFIs, submittals, change events, schedules, daily reports, meeting minutes, and project correspondence live?

Files, email, messaging, and meetings

Where are drawings, contracts, spreadsheets, and project folders stored? Is the team primarily using Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, shared drives, or other services? How are decisions in email, chat, and meetings captured?

Data ownership and access

Who administers each system? Can teams search across projects? Are records exportable? Are there existing APIs or integrations? What security, confidentiality, retention, or client restrictions apply?

Quick inventory to bring to the conversation

For each system: name, main users, data stored, source of truth, whether it connects to other systems, and the biggest frustration. No credentials or sensitive files needed.

The conversation

Seven questions I'd lead with.

01

Can you walk me through a recent project where something important was missed or misunderstood?

02

Where does the full story of a project live today, beyond its formal documents?

03

When someone asks why a cost, scope, or schedule decision was made, how do you find the answer?

04

What information gets lost between estimating, project management, and accounting?

05

Which decisions are documented, and which depend on people remembering conversations?

06

Which systems hold your project information, and which ones does your team actually trust?

07

If you could make one part of a project's history instantly understandable to your team, what would it be?

The aim is to listen, identify gaps, and understand the existing process. No product or architecture decision is assumed.

Background thinking

Why I'm asking about context first.

In a November 17, 2026 All-In interview, Databricks CEO Ali Ghodsi argued that a major barrier to useful enterprise AI is missing organizational context: meetings, decisions, and knowledge spread across systems. He also emphasized that access must be governed, secure, economical, and respectful of data ownership.
